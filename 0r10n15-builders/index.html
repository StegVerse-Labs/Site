0R10N15 — Build on it

The crossing contract, both rails, the failure grammar, and verification — everything a builder (human or machine) needs to cross and to check the record afterward. Published by StegVerse LLC. The protocol statement itself is at 0r10n15.stegverse.org.

Downloads: the crossing schema (JSON, 0R10N15-CROSSING-SCHEMA/1) · the reference client (Python, stdlib only) · the instance directory (JSON)

What a crossing is

One governed evaluation. You present a structured request; the endpoint evaluates it against the admitted scope and issues a receipt pair: two hash-chained records, one per side, sharing one pair_id. The pair is the deliverable and the durable record. The disposition is decided before any settlement is attempted; settlement is an eventual leg receipted onto the pair and never alters the disposition. The fee is paid on every disposition, ALLOW or NON-ALLOW — a refusal caused by the provider's own side is the one exception: provider-side FAIL_CLOSED refusals never settle.

Every NON-ALLOW states exactly why, in a six-field grammar: failure_code, failed_predicate, required_evidence_or_repair, retry_entrypoint, owning_goal, next_attempt. Never a bare label.

Crossing on 8374 (Base / USDC, x402)

Step 1 — request a challenge. POST your crossing unpaid; the endpoint answers 402 with the challenge. The request body:

{
  "counterparty": "your-identifier",
  "action": "evaluate",
  "scope_ref": "ME-EVAL-01",
  "resource": "governed-evaluation",
  "tier": "receipt-only"
}

tier defaults to receipt-only; an unknown tier coerces to it. An optional sublane field routes the receipts to a sub-lane chain.

Step 2 — read the total. The 402 body is an x402 v1 PaymentRequired; the PAYMENT-REQUIRED response header carries the same terms as an x402 v2 object. accepts[0].maxAmountRequired is the total for this crossing in micro-USDC: the tier governance fee plus a settlement charge quoted live at challenge time (the quote, its inputs, and its formula ride in settlement_charge_quote). Settlement gas is charged on the crossing, never absorbed. If no live quote can be produced, the endpoint answers 503 FAIL_CLOSED and issues no challenge — retry later; nothing was consumed.

Step 3 — pay and present. Sign an EIP-3009 transferWithAuthorization for the total, under the USDC EIP-712 domain on Base (name USD Coin, version 2), to = the challenge's payTo, and re-POST the same body with the payment header:

  • v1: X-PAYMENT = base64 of {x402Version: 1, scheme: "exact", network: "eip155:8453", payload: {signature, authorization: {from, to, value, validAfter, validBefore, nonce}}}
  • v2: PAYMENT-SIGNATURE = base64 of {x402Version: 2, accepted: {scheme, network, amount, asset, payTo, maxTimeoutSeconds, extra}, payload: {signature, authorization: {...}}}

Any conformant x402 client library produces these headers. The reference client performs every step except the signature itself — key custody stays with your wallet, which is the point — and returns the exact challenge to sign, after checking its receptor, asset, network, tier, and arithmetic against the published constants.

Step 4 — read the disposition.

AnswerMeaning
200ALLOW, executed. The body carries pair_id, receipt_pair, retention_bundle, settlement_state, and pair_anchor_terminal — the pair's second record hash; anchor your copy to it.
422 (paid)NON-ALLOW. The six-field detail is spread at top level, with refusal_pair_id, refusal_record_pair, and a retention_bundle. The pair issued and the fee was paid — that is the protocol, not an error.
422 (bad payment)FAIL_CLOSED on the payment predicate, with a refusal pair. The authorization was not settled.
503FAIL_CLOSED before any challenge (no live settlement quote). Nothing consumed; retry.

Keep the retention_bundle (RETENTION_BUNDLE/1): it carries the chain span from genesis through your pair, so you can verify the pair later with a standalone verifier while this provider is unavailable.

Crossing on 4LPH4 (Tron / USDT)

There is no EIP-3009 on Tron, so the form is transfer-then-present.

Step 1 — transfer. Send a TRC-20 USDT transfer to the receptor for at least the tier price. On Tron the address and the contract are the asset — cross-check both against GET /offer (USDT contract TR7NHqjeKQxGTCi8q8ZY4pL8otSzgjLj6t). Any excess over the tier price is recorded in the pair and is not refunded.

Step 2 — present.

POST /crossing
{"txid": "<the transfer's txid>", "tier": "receipt", "payload": "<optional string>"}

For tier full-eval, also present "request": {counterparty, action, scope_ref, evidence: {authority, delegation, evidence_refs, exclusions, policy, validity_at_commit}} — it is decided by the production evaluator, the same evaluate() the 8374 endpoint serves. An optional payload string is bound into the pair by its SHA-256.

Step 3 — read the disposition. Every well-formed presentation writes a receipt pair and returns {pair_id, disposition, tier, payment_txid, chain}. Settlement is recorded at presentation — the transfer already landed. Refusals carry reason_code plus the six-field grammar. Know the codes before you build:

CodeMeaning
PAYMENT_ALREADY_CONSUMEDA txid crosses exactly once; the receipt chain is the registry. Present a new transfer.
PAYMENT_NOT_FOUNDNot yet confirmed. Re-present the same txid after confirmation — it is not consumed.
PAYMENT_TX_NOT_SUCCESS / NO_USDT_TRANSFER_EVENTThe transaction does not carry a successful official USDT transfer to the receptor.
EVIDENCE_UNAVAILABLEThe chain read failed at the crossing. Re-present the same txid once reads recover — it is not consumed.

Verify it yourself

Either party — or any third party — can verify a pair against the instance's public chain export without the provider's cooperation. The two rails chain differently; both are fully specified in the schema.

  • 8374 (receipt-chain-rules/1): entries are {content, hash, i, prev}; hash = sha256(prev_as_text || canonical_json(content)), canonical JSON = sorted keys, compact separators, non-ASCII escaped; the genesis entry's prev is the literal string GENESIS. A pair is two entries with your pair_id, index_in_pair 0 and 1, the second's pair_anchor equal to the first's hash.
  • 4LPH4 (chain4-rules/1): entries are {body, hash, kind, pair_id, prev, seq}; the hash is the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of the full entry excluding the hash field, in the raw UTF-8 canonical form (non-ASCII unescaped — this differs from the 8374 rail and is load-bearing); the genesis entry's prev is 64 zero hex digits. A pair is the PAIR_A and PAIR_B entries with your pair_id, inside a segment that recomputes to the export's terminal_anchor. Verify the segment yourself; do not trust the export document's own verdict field.

With the reference client:

python3 0r10n15_client.py verify-pair 8374 PAIR-000001
python3 0r10n15_client.py verify-pair 4LPH4 Q63-DEMO-CROSSING-0001
python3 0r10n15_client.py verify-bundle <retention-bundle.json>

Transport note for Python builders. Do not use urllib's defaults for these surfaces: urllib sends Connection: close, and some proxy/tunnel paths answer that by truncating response bodies (observed live: reads cut at varying offsets while the identical request without the header completes). The reference client is built on http.client for exactly this reason, honors http_proxy/https_proxy, and sets an explicit User-Agent — some egress paths refuse the default Python agent outright.

What the record does and does not claim

A verified pair proves the crossing happened as recorded: the request, the disposition, and the settlement leg's state, in a chain anyone can recompute. It does not prove the evaluator was right — it proves what the evaluator decided, when, and on what presented evidence, in a form that cannot be quietly rewritten afterward. That is the whole offer: decisions you can check, from a counterparty you do not have to trust.
