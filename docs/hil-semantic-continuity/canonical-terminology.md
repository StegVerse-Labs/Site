# HIL Semantic Continuity — Canonical Terminology v0.1

## Status

Repository-owned foundational terminology for `HIL_SEMANTIC_CONTINUITY`.

## Terms

- **Existence**: primitive presence; does not depend on continuity, governance, observation, or reconstruction.
- **State**: a bounded description of an identity or system under declared observational conditions.
- **Governance**: the constraint-bearing observational structure by which states become distinguishable, identity is established or carried through transition, and continuity becomes meaningful.
- **Identity**: the governed basis for determining whether state descriptions refer to the same entity, system, or admissible successor.
- **Continuity**: a governed relationship among states of an identity. Governance informs continuity; without governance, continuity and discontinuity are not distinguishable.
- **Transition**: a governed relation between states that may preserve, transform, split, merge, terminate, or fail to establish identity.
- **Trajectory**: an ordered relational structure inferred from governed comparisons among states of an identity.
- **Observer**: the entity or mechanism performing a declared comparison. Within this formalism, governance is the observer structure, not merely something an observer chooses to apply.
- **Reconstruction**: interpretation of preserved state and transition evidence sufficient to recover an admissible account of identity, constraints, confidence, and transformation.
- **Interoperability layer**: any human, AI, institutional, technical, journalistic, legal, scientific, or other layer that receives one representation and emits another.
- **Semantic continuity**: preservation of enough meaning and structure for the source reasoning to remain independently reconstructable after transformation.
- **Boundary**: the declared scope of entities, events, time range, geography, causal chain, or system included in a proposition.
- **Confidence**: an explicitly declared degree of support or uncertainty attached to a proposition.
- **Entropy-ledger hypothesis**: the research hypothesis that physical record formation incurs entropy cost and reconstruction interprets the resulting ledger subject to governance and admissibility.
- **Singularity limit hypothesis**: the research hypothesis that the singularity may be modeled as a limit at which admissible relational structure becomes maximally reconstructable to a capable observer.

## Claim classes

- **Observation**: a bounded report of perceived, measured, or recorded conditions.
- **Hypothesis**: a testable proposed explanation or relationship with unresolved truth value.
- **Inference**: a proposition derived from observations, assumptions, or other propositions.
- **Conclusion**: a proposition represented as supported by the completed scope of an analysis.
- **Analogy**: a comparison used to illuminate structure without establishing identity or proof.
- **Compatibility statement**: a claim that two formalisms can coexist without a demonstrated contradiction under declared assumptions.
- **Conjecture**: a formally stated proposition not yet proven.
- **Axiom**: a declared primitive or starting assumption internal to the formalism.
- **Definition**: a binding use of a term inside the formalism.
- **Established external result**: a result attributed to an identified external discipline and source, not produced by this formalism.

## Prohibited transformations

Without an explicit receipt and justification, an interoperability layer must not transform:

- observation into belief attribution;
- hypothesis into assertion;
- probability into certainty or dismissal;
- investigation request into proof demand;
- declared boundary into a narrower or broader boundary;
- source identity into a substituted identity;
- compatibility into confirmation;
- analogy into physical evidence;
- conjecture into established result.

## Activation rule

Terminology is foundational infrastructure, not scientific validation. Activation requires deterministic receipts, fixtures, validators, reconstruction testing, and explicit uncertainty handling.
