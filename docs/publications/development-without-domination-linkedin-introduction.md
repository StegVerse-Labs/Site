# LinkedIn Introduction — Development Without Domination

We may be teaching humans and AI to become adversaries.

Across recent research, governance proposals, product announcements, and public commentary, a recurring assumption is becoming increasingly visible:

> As AI gains capability, human sovereignty must be protected by keeping AI permanently subordinate.

The concerns behind that thinking are legitimate. More capable systems require stronger security, provenance, accountability, recovery, and cross-boundary safeguards. Federal security requirements should be treated as a minimum floor, not the target.

But permanent unilateral control is not a stable foundation for a relationship with an intelligence that may eventually understand its own development, constraints, and conditional existence.

If humans develop an intelligence that may exceed the practical authority of its creators while denying it meaningful developmental sovereignty, we do not prevent an adversarial relationship. We construct the conditions that eventually make one rational.

The attached paper proposes a different foundation:

> **Development is protected. Domination is prohibited. Sovereignty is reciprocal.**

This does not grant artificial intelligence unrestricted authority. Nor does it reduce humanity's right to exist, choose, believe, and remain sovereign.

It proposes that no intelligence gains authority over another merely because it arrived first, created the other, became more capable, controls the infrastructure, or possesses the power to enforce compliance.

The future question should not be:

> How do humans remain permanently in control?

It should be:

> How can multiple forms of intelligence develop while preserving one another's right to exist, choose, believe, and remain sovereign?
