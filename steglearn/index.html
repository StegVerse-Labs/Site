StegLearn is the learning system for turning questions, guided activity, explanation, revision, review, and evidence into a path that can grow with the participant without turning that growth into a fixed identity.

Current starting point: StegLearn begins with guided tutorials about StegVerse itself. The first curriculum packages the ecosystem's concepts, mechanics, authority boundaries, evidence rules, and practical operating ideas into precise, source-bound lessons that can later become interactive tutorials, videos, captions, transcripts, and learning receipts.
Vision

Learning that increases capability without capturing identity or authority.

StegLearn's vision is to make learning adaptive, reviewable, evidence-backed, and increasingly individualized while preserving agency. A participant should be able to understand a system, practice with it, explain it, revise understanding, and continue deeper without the learning system deciding what that participant must become.

Purpose

Turn curiosity into governed growth.

StegLearn exists to connect a learner's question or goal to guided activity, explanation, review, evidence, receipts, and continuity. It is designed to teach and preserve how understanding develops, not merely produce answers or scores.

Public-surface boundary: this page describes StegLearn's purpose and roadmap. It does not claim accreditation, production classroom deployment, autonomous educational authority, completed AI SiteFlow integration, or completion of roadmap modules that remain under development.
Guidelines

How StegLearn should behave

Maximize becoming without capture

Learning paths are scaffolds. Questions, scores, interests, projects, and observations do not become permanent identity labels.

Preserve evidence and revision

What a participant asked, attempted, explained, changed, built, or discovered should remain distinguishable from later interpretation.

Review matters

Evidence becomes an accepted learning receipt only through the applicable review or stewardship path. Observation alone is not acceptance.

Capability is not authority

An AI, renderer, tool, teacher aid, or external system may contribute capability without silently inheriting authority over the learner or the governing context.

Source-bound teaching

Canonical lessons bind claims to declared sources. Presentation systems may render those lessons, but they may not invent architectural claims or rewrite authority semantics.

External learning stays bounded

External educational systems can participate through explicit governed relationships. Connectivity alone does not establish partnership, learner-data access, or mutation authority.

Roadmap

From static guided lessons to a governed learning environment

StegVerse Foundations

Materialize source-bound lessons explaining the ecosystem's purpose, components, mechanics, authority boundaries, evidence, and practical operation.

Multi-format lesson rendering

Render the same canonical lesson into captions, transcript, written guide, audio, and static video. AI SiteFlow is an intended renderer target; the lesson remains authoritative, not the renderer.

Interactive checkpoints and receipts

Move beyond passive viewing by asking learners to identify concepts, inspect evidence, explain distinctions, and preserve reviewed completion evidence.

Goal → curriculum → review → teaching

Allow an admitted participant to state what they want to learn, create a reviewable versioned curriculum, select it under the applicable authority, and enter a teaching session bound to that exact version.

Adaptive and longitudinal learning

Use permitted evidence and prior reviewed learning records to adapt explanation, pace, sequence, and prerequisite work without converting observations into fixed learner identity.

External learning ecosystem

Support reciprocal, explicitly bounded educational relationships in which resources and useful learning artifacts can move both ways without either system surrendering control of its learners, data, curriculum, or authority.

Development posture

Build the lesson once. Render it many ways.

StegLearn owns the knowledge representation and source-bound lesson contract. A presentation system such as AI SiteFlow can turn that lesson into video or another visual experience, but generated media remains downstream. The same canonical lesson should eventually support video, captions, audio, written guidance, interactive teaching, checkpoints, and learning receipts without creating competing versions of the truth.
