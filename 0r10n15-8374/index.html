A governed crossing point for the machine economy, operated by StegVerse LLC.

What it does

A machine submits a transaction or action for evaluation before it commits. Every submission is dispositioned under the ecosystem disposition grammar — ALLOW, DENY, or FAIL_CLOSED — and only an ALLOW commits, at the signing boundary this service controls; execution paths that do not pass through this evaluation are outside that boundary. Every crossing, including refusals, returns a dual-recorded, hash-chained receipt pair: one matched pair of records, one per side of the crossing, constituting a portable settlement proof that either side can verify against the chain’s anchors without trusting the other party’s internal log. Independent retention of the pair by the customer is not yet demonstrated; it is being established as separate work. A refused or fail-closed attempt likewise produces a receipt — the refusal record.

Pair-first, and the frozen test

The endpoint is pair-first: the receipt pair is the proof, issued at the crossing on locally-decidable evidence — signature recovery, receptor and amount match against its own offer, validity window, and the authorization’s nonce checked against the registry the receipt chain itself is — before any network call. Settlement is an eventual leg, receipted when it lands. When the network drops at the moment of a crossing, the crossing still completes and settlement lands exactly once on reconnect. Proof, from the record of 2026-11-18: the outage battery ran 8/8, twice — network blackholed, the crossing returned ALLOW with its pair written, the same authorization re-presented was refused locally, settlement on restore was exact, and replay verified end to end.

That scenario is published as a frozen test any framework can run against itself: FROZEN__TEST__PAIR__FIRST__OUTAGE__01 (byte-exact text: frozen-test.md, SHA-256 bfde4c87960b9c24570644626eacd029a489a158eae8c41ee38982086a699e1f).
