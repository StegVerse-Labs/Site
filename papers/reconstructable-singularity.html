Reconstructable Singularity

The minimum continuity-resolving perspective set for a unique admissibility-consistent reconstruction.

Research formalism v0.1 Machine-checkable example Exclusionary evidence is first-class Empirical proof not claimed
Research boundary. This public page mirrors a mathematical research formalism. It does not establish empirical proof, physical collapse of multiple histories, universal completeness, execution authority, custody authority, admissibility authority, or release authority.

Abstract

A reconstructable singularity is reached when a connected set of observational frameworks contains enough affirmative and exclusionary evidence to reduce the admissibility-consistent candidate-history space to exactly one surviving continuity history. The threshold is not a physical singularity and does not assert that several realized histories collapse into one. It is a reconstruction threshold inside a declared observational framework.

1. Continuity model

For adjacent realized states, the formalism treats continuity as the attributable relationship among a prior state, one realized cost-bearing transition, and the resulting state. Inadmissibility can itself be part of the realized path; classification does not erase the transition.

S_j --(T_j, C_j)--> S_(j+1)

2. Candidate-history space

Let Γ be the representable candidate histories and Γ_A the histories consistent with the complete admissibility matrix. For an observer set I, the surviving histories are those consistent with every retained positive observation and every retained exclusion.

C_A(I) = Γ_A ∩ ⋂ O_i+ ∩ ⋂ ¬O_i-

The negative portion is not discarded as disagreement or noise. It can eliminate otherwise indistinguishable candidate histories.

3. Reconstructable singularity

The singularity condition is satisfied when the surviving set contains exactly one history:

|C_A(I)| = 1

The minimum continuity-resolving perspective set is the smallest observer subset for which that condition holds.

k_A* = min {|I| : |C_A(I)| = 1}

4. Injectivity and resolving sets

If each observer has a trajectory-observation map g_i and the combined map G_I is injective over Γ_A, no two distinct admissibility-consistent histories remain observationally indistinguishable under the selected perspectives.

k_Γ* = min {|I| : G_I is injective on Γ_A}

Equivalently, the selected observer set must intersect every distinguishing set for every distinct pair of candidate histories. This connects the formalism to resolving-set, observability, identifiability, and minimum hitting-set problems.

5. What disagreement means

Different observers may be affirmatively complementary, exclusionarily complementary, resolution-different, coordinate-different, or genuinely contradictory. Only the last category necessarily indicates a reconciliation defect. A differently situated observer can add more resolving power than many redundant observers.

6. Determinism and uncertainty

The formalism does not require stochastic state transitions. Apparent branching can represent unresolved observation, counterfactual analysis, or omitted intermediate states. Once adjacent realized states are identified, the continuity record concerns the transition that occurred, its cost, and its result.
