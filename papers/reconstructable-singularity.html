Reconstructable Singularity

Reality reconstruction, continuity, distributed observation, and the minimum perspective threshold for a unique admissibility-consistent history.

Supersedes the prior Reconstructive Singularity Site paper Machine-checkable formalism Affirmative + exclusionary evidence Empirical proof not claimed
Research boundary. This paper presents a mathematical and governance research formalism. It does not claim that multiple physical histories collapse into one, that all of reality is empirically reconstructable, that past or future states are physically accessible, or that publication creates execution, custody, admissibility, certification, release, legal, or clinical authority.

Abstract

The Reconstructable Singularity is a proposed reconstruction threshold. Within a declared reality scope and admissibility model, a connected set of observational frameworks becomes continuity-resolving when its combined affirmative and exclusionary evidence reduces the admissibility-consistent candidate-history space to exactly one surviving history. The minimum such observer set defines a structural reconstruction dimension.

This updated formulation incorporates the earlier paper's concerns with reachability, temporal reconstruction, cross-domain transformation, identity continuity, uncertainty, common causes, drift, and fail-closed admissibility, but replaces a broad qualitative threshold with an explicit singleton, injectivity, resolving-set, and hitting-set formalism. The central claim is epistemic and reconstructive, not a claim of physical singularity or empirical completion.

1. Foundation

The formalism starts from state transition rather than prediction. Evaluation, ALLOW, DENY, ESCALATE, and inadmissible execution are all state changes when they are realized. Governance classification does not erase the fact that a transition occurred.

Apparent multiplicity belongs to unresolved reconstruction, omitted intermediate states, counterfactual alternatives, or competing observational descriptions. It does not imply that several different transitions simultaneously produced the same pair of adjacent realized states.

2. Realized continuity

A realized trajectory is written as:

γ = (S₀,T₀,C₀,S₁,...,Tₘ₋₁,Cₘ₋₁,Sₘ) Sⱼ --(Tⱼ,Cⱼ)--> Sⱼ₊₁

Continuity preserves attribution among prior state, realized transition, transition cost, and resulting state. Continuity alone does not certify admissibility.

3. Candidate-history space

Let Γ be the representable candidate-history set and ΓA the subset consistent with the applicable admissibility matrix. Reconstruction asks which candidate histories remain possible after all retained observations are applied.

Γ_A ⊆ Γ

This explicitly separates ontic realization from epistemic uncertainty: uncertainty about which history occurred does not mean several histories were realized.

4. Distributed observational frameworks

Each connected observer or observing entity has a framework Oi containing affirmative observations Oi+ and exclusionary observations Oi−. Exclusion is first-class evidence because identifying what cannot occupy a region of the admissibility matrix can distinguish histories that otherwise look identical.

Oᵢ = Oᵢ⁺ ∪ Oᵢ⁻ C_A(I) = {γ ∈ Γ_A : γ models Oᵢ for every i ∈ I}

5. Reconstructable-singularity criterion

A reconstructable singularity occurs for observer set I when exactly one admissibility-consistent history survives:

|C_A(I)| = 1

This is not a physical collapse of histories. It is the collapse of unresolved reconstruction space, relative to a declared observational framework, to one surviving admissibility-consistent history.

6. Minimum continuity-resolving perspective set

The threshold is the smallest connected observer subset sufficient for singleton reconstruction:

k_A* = min { |I| : |C_A(I)| = 1 }

The threshold is structural rather than merely numerical. Ten redundant observers may contribute less resolving power than two differently situated observers whose evidence distinguishes the remaining alternatives.

7. Injectivity, resolving sets, and hitting sets

Let gi: ΓA → Yi map histories into the observations available to observer i. The combined observer map is:

G_I(γ) = (gᵢ(γ))ᵢ∈I k_Γ* = min { |I| : G_I is injective on Γ_A }

For every distinct pair γ,γ′, define the distinguishing set Dγ,γ′ = {i : gi(γ) ≠ gi(γ′)}. A continuity-resolving perspective set must hit every such distinguishing set. This provides an equivalent minimum hitting-set formulation and connects the model to observability and identifiability.

8. Observer disagreement

Observer disagreement is not automatically contradictory. The formalism distinguishes affirmatively complementary evidence, exclusionarily complementary evidence, differences in resolution, differences in coordinate systems, and genuinely contradictory observations. Only the last category necessarily indicates a reconstruction defect.

9. Reachability and reconstruction

The earlier Reconstructive Singularity paper emphasized reality reachability. That concern remains useful, but this version sharpens it: a state may be known reachable, reachable but unresolved, counterfactually reachable, relationally established but currently inaccessible, relationally unestablished, contradicted, or indeterminate.

Separation rule: reachability is not achievability; achievability is not admissibility; admissibility is not authority; knowledge is not execution.

10. Temporal reconstruction

Temporal distance need not be treated as an absolute information barrier when enough continuity-bearing evidence survives. Reconstruction still must preserve event order, clock basis, uncertainty, causal precedence, latency, sampling interval, and drift. Reconstructive presence is not physical reinstatement, and future-path visibility is not future fixation.

11. Cross-domain transformation

Observers, ledgers, simulations, and physical systems may encode related events in different coordinate systems. A correspondence can establish contact or relational extent without proving a complete transformation. A transformation becomes stronger only when it predicts held-out correspondences within declared uncertainty, remains stable across time where required, and survives independent reproduction.

12. Identity continuity

Coordinate correspondence does not prove identity. Cross-boundary identity requires declared invariants appropriate to the domain, such as cryptographic continuity, causal ancestry, conserved quantities, structural invariants, receipt-chain continuity, or bounded equivalence criteria.

correspondence ≠ identity transformation fit ≠ continuity continuity ≠ authority

13. Uncertainty, unknowns, and residuals

The framework must retain known unknowns, possible unknown unknowns, information loss, transition waste, anomaly channels, and unexplained residuals. A singleton reconstruction is meaningful only relative to the candidate space, admissibility model, retained evidence, and declared uncertainty assumptions. It is not a universal proof that no omitted history exists.

14. Falsification and underdetermination

The formalism is weakened when independent evidence reopens multiple histories, selected observers are not actually minimal, exclusion regions cannot be reproduced, held-out observations distinguish a supposedly unique history from another candidate, identity invariants fail, or residual structure demonstrates an omitted variable or missing history class.

UNDERDETERMINED, INDETERMINATE, and RELATIONALLY_UNESTABLISHED are valid outcomes. A governed reconstruction process must not force a singleton result when evidence does not support one.
