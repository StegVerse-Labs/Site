Coherent Life and Admissible Existence — Companion Extensions

Notation, Theorem Witnesses, and Two Representational Tests

Companion to Coherent Life and Admissible Existence · Canonical research owner: Admissible-Existence/AE

Publication boundary. This companion does not replace or rewrite the original Coherent Life working paper. It conjoins three later documents: the notation/theorem-witness supplement, Empirical Addendum I, and Empirical/Theoretical Addendum II.

Part I · Formal Companion

Notation Table and Theorem Witnesses

The formal companion supplies a single-source symbol table for overloaded notation and explicit constructions for theorem candidates that can be witnessed directly. Its purpose is to prevent symbol drift, distinguish target sets from recovery basins, and retain proof-ready constructions without changing the identity of the parent working paper.

Among its corrections, purpose potential is separated from the transition map; support-production terms are separated from presence; the viability-constraint count, receipt-chain hash, and individuation weights receive non-colliding notation; and coherent target regions remain distinct from coherent recovery basins.

Part II · Empirical Addendum I

Unknown-Class Transformation at the Quantum-Gravitational Boundary

Empirical boundary. The cited quantum equivalence-principle experiment is used as a bounded real-world case. It does not empirically validate Admissible Existence, prove quantum gravity, or establish unification.

The extension treats incomplete knowledge as represented state rather than absence of state. It separates established knowledge, unresolved classes, observations, interpretations, and governing constraints so that evidence can change the structure of uncertainty without being promoted beyond what the observation supports.

Observation ≠ Interpretation ≠ Established knowledge
Observation → Constraint → Unknown transformation → Admissible interpretation

Its central preservation requirement is that a system retain the observation, unresolved possibility space, rejected alternatives, scope conditions, and inference path needed to reconstruct how a claim arose.

Part III · Empirical/Theoretical Addendum II

Recoverable Capacity Across Representational Boundaries

Theoretical boundary. The 2026 cavity-QED thermodynamic framework is used as a physical/theoretical test of whether a representation preserves distinctions that materially alter reachable futures. It is not empirical validation of the complete AE theory.

The extension distinguishes boundary crossing from annihilation. A component can leave a declared subsystem while retaining physically realizable transformations, usable capacity, or work potential. A coarse terminal label may therefore destroy information that is necessary for later reconstruction or for reproducing a valid limiting regime.

transition-then-project ≈ project-then-transition

A reduced representation is deficient when it collapses states whose materially relevant reachable futures differ.

Joint Result

Preserve distinctions that change claims or reachable futures

The two addenda expose complementary representation failures:

  • Addendum I: new evidence must not cause unsupported promotion from observation to certainty.
  • Addendum II: boundary crossing or abstraction must not cause destructive collapse of distinct successor capacities.
Combined preservation principle. An admissible representation should preserve every distinction required to reconstruct materially different claims or materially different future transitions.
