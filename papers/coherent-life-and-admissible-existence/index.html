Admissible Existence · Conjoined Working Formal Paper + Empirical Application

Coherent Life and Admissible Existence

A Coupled Theory of Boundaries, Recoverability, Authority, Purpose, and Continued Coherence

Canonical research owner: Admissible-Existence/AE · Public Site projection: StegVerse-Labs/Site

Abstract. Admissible Existence studies when a state, entity, system, observer, process, or domain is not merely present, but remains coherently identifiable across transition without destroying the conditions required for its own continuation or the continuation of coupled entities. The theory distinguishes presence, individuation, coherence, transition capacity, viability, plural recoverability, observability, reconstructability, compositional compatibility, authority, purpose, and relational admissibility.

The central thesis is that existence is not merely presence. A coherent existence claim requires a candidate entity to remain sufficiently itself while change continues, and admissible existence adds the condition that the entity not preserve itself through transitions that destroy the wider conditions under which coherent existence remains possible.

Existence becomes formally meaningful when presence persists coherently through admissible transition.

What the paper formalizes

The paper develops a joint state-space treatment of coherent life under bounded interaction, including boundary coherence, physical and authority recoverability, purpose and continuity recoverability, joint relational admissibility, compositional compatibility, and reconstructability.

For entity i, the state is represented as an ordered structure containing internal organization, boundary configuration, resource substrate, retained history, authority configuration, purpose configuration, and admissibility/relational standing. The theory then asks whether transitions preserve the conditions required for coherent continuation rather than merely whether a system remains detectable.

Scope and non-claims

The paper applies its formalism to biological, artificial, institutional, computational, social, and hybrid systems. It does not claim that every persistent system is alive, that every coherent system is conscious, that physical viability proves legitimate authority, that local stability proves global admissibility, or that mathematical analogy constitutes empirical proof.

Empirical Application I · Quantum–Gravitational Boundary

Unknown-Class Transformation at the Quantum-Gravitational Boundary

Empirical boundary. This section does not claim that the cited experiment validates Admissible Existence. It uses the experiment as a bounded real-world case for examining whether observation, interpretation, uncertainty, and successor-state distinctions can be preserved without collapsing them into one declaration.

Observation and bounded inference

In 2026, researchers from Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, the University of Ulm, the University of Oxford, and collaborators reported a direct measurement of the predicted quantum phase associated with a freely falling quantum object. Ultracold rubidium atoms were placed into quantum superposition and recombined interferometrically after different gravitational histories. The measured phase was consistent with the prediction obtained by applying Einstein's equivalence principle to the tested quantum system.

The result is narrower than several claims that might be attached to it. It does not demonstrate that gravity itself is quantized and does not provide a unified theory of quantum mechanics and gravitation. The empirical result constrains a tested possibility space while leaving substantial unresolved structure intact.

Epistemic state representation

S_t = (K_t, U_t, O_t, I_t, C_t)

Here K represents established knowledge, U unresolved or unknown classes, O observations and evidence, I supported or proposed interpretations, and C constraints governing admissible interpretation and transition.

Let U_EP,Q,t represent uncertainty concerning the behavior of the equivalence principle when applied to matter exhibiting genuinely quantum behavior. Prior to observation, an abstract possibility domain can include consistency, deviation, failure, and unrepresented behavior.

O_(t+1) = O_t ∪ {e_QGI}
Ω_(t+1) ⊂ Ω_t

The observation constrains the prior possibility domain because detectable deviations within the tested regime become less compatible with the observed result.

Information gain without total resolution

IG_t = H(U_t) - H(U_(t+1)) > 0
H(U_(t+1)) ≠ 0

The unresolved domain changes without disappearing. The transition is therefore not U_EP,Q → K(quantum gravity) and not U_EP,Q → K(unification of GR and QM). A bounded representation instead records consistency in the tested regime while preserving unresolved propositions concerning gravitational quantization, stronger-field behavior, mass scale, coupling, and unification.

Observation is not interpretation

Observation ≠ Interpretation ≠ Established knowledge
O → I does not imply I → K

A proposed epistemic successor should therefore be evaluated by an admissibility relation. The claim that the measured phase is consistent with the equivalence principle in the tested quantum regime is supported by the experiment. The broader claims that gravity has been demonstrated to be quantum or that quantum mechanics and general relativity have been unified are not established by the same evidence.

Unknown-class refinement

Scientific progress can reduce uncertainty while simultaneously producing more explicitly represented unresolved questions. A coarse unresolved class such as U(quantum matter–gravity relation) may partition into separate unresolved classes for gravitational quantization, mass scale, superposition duration, strong-field behavior, coupling, and unification.

H(U_(t+1)) < H(U_t) while |U_(t+1)| > |U_t|

There is no contradiction: the state can become better resolved even while the number of explicitly represented unknown classes increases.

Structure of the unresolved domain

ΔU_t = (ΔH_t, ΔT_t)

ΔH_t describes change in uncertainty magnitude; ΔT_t describes change in the internal structure, partition, or relational organization of the unresolved domain. At this stage, T(U) means unknown-space structure, not a claim that a strict mathematical topology has already been defined.

Φ_U : (U_t, E_t, C_t) → (K_(t+1), U_(t+1))
Φ_U ∈ {contract, expand, partition, merge, resolve, instantiate}

The quantum-equivalence experiment is principally a combination of contraction and partition: it constrains one previously broad possibility space while exposing a sharper collection of still-unresolved questions.

Empirical consequence for Admissible Existence

A real scientific measurement can be represented as an admissible state transition in which new observation alters both uncertainty magnitude and the structure of represented unknowns without requiring unresolved propositions to be promoted prematurely to established knowledge.
Observation → Constraint → Unknown transformation → Admissible interpretation

This is preferable to an epistemic collapse of Observation → Unqualified certainty, because the latter discards information needed to reconstruct how a claim arose and what remained unresolved.

R_(t→t+1) = {S_t, E_t, C_t, Φ_U, I_t, S_(t+1)}

Knowledge advancement is transformation of represented possibility, not merely accumulation of declarations.

Falsifiable implication

If scientific state transitions cannot be meaningfully distinguished by preserving separate observation, interpretation, constraint, and unresolved-state objects—or if collapsing these distinctions consistently preserves everything required for later reconstruction—then this part of the Admissible Existence formalism provides no explanatory or operational advantage. Conversely, whenever reconstruction requires information that a collapsed representation would have discarded, the distinction has operational value.
