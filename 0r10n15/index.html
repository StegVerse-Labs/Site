0R10N15

The index of the systems under the 0R10N15 purview, operated by StegVerse LLC. One protocol, stated identically in every instance; the instances differ only where they state the instance. Every entry below carries its own verification path — an entry a visitor cannot check from this page is a claim, and claims are not the product.

What this is, plainly

A crossing is a checkpoint for a machine payment. Before value moves, the payment is evaluated against a published policy; whatever the decision — allow, deny, or fail closed — both sides leave with a matched, hash-chained receipt pair that either side can re-verify later without trusting the other. Example: a buying agent takes a receipt-tier crossing at 8374 and is quoted a total of 0.002103 USDC — the 0.0005 USDC governance fee plus the live-quoted settlement charge. It pays; the crossing returns ALLOW with its receipt pair, and the pair’s place in the chain can be recomputed by anyone from the published export, today or next year, without asking the operator.

The protocol

A machine submits a transaction or action for evaluation before it commits. Every submission is dispositioned under the ecosystem disposition grammar — ALLOW, DENY, or FAIL_CLOSED — and only an ALLOW commits, at the signing boundary the service controls; execution paths that do not pass through this evaluation are outside that boundary. Every crossing, including refusals, returns a dual-recorded, hash-chained receipt pair: one matched pair of records, one per side of the crossing, constituting a portable settlement proof that either side can verify against the chain’s anchors without trusting the other party’s internal log. Pair issuance follows the rail. On Base (8374), the pair is issued locally and unconditionally at the crossing, before any network call; settlement is an eventual leg, receipted when it lands. On Tron (4LPH4), the crossing is transfer-then-present: the payment lands first, and the pair records the evaluation of the landed payment evidence. A refused or fail-closed attempt likewise produces a receipt — the refusal record. Independent retention of the pair by the customer is not yet demonstrated; it is being established as separate work.

Availability is a property of the crossing, not of a destination: the 0R10N15 protocol is to be available at every crossing, on every rail.

The one promise

There is only one promise in this purview: evidence, in receipted interactions. No promise of revenue, outcomes, or adoption is made anywhere on this page or in any instance under it.

What the chains prove — and what they do not

The chains are tamper-evident: every record is hash-linked to the one before it, so altering a retained record breaks the chain at that point, and any reader holding an export can recompute the whole chain and find the break. Today the terminal anchors are served from the operator’s own surfaces, so a reader verifies consistency against the anchor the operator publishes; checkpoint anchoring of the anchors on a public blockchain is not in place. The proof, as it stands, is tamper-evidence against a retained anchor — not independent timestamping.

Dependencies and failure modes

The crossings read the chains they settle on. Base crossings read Base through public RPC endpoints and quote the settlement charge from live gas conditions; Tron crossings read Tron through TronGrid. When a read is unavailable, a crossing does not guess: it fails closed and receipts the refusal. The first two crossings presented on the Tron rail stand on its chain as exactly that record — FAIL_CLOSED, EVIDENCE_UNAVAILABLE — written when the chain read was down, before the read was repaired.

Admission to this index

An entry appears here when a system carries the crossing standard as a live instance — a market where crossings occur. Components of an instance are named inside their instance’s entry, not as entries. Future rails join when live, never when planned; absent cells stay absent.

The on-ramp — in build, not live

An automated framework analysis and suite generation is in build: a framework’s evidence structure is determined — where its claims live, what receipts them, what a reviewer can replay — its full product is generated as a suite that proves conformance inside itself, and the product is born connected to the protocol, its interactions receipted from birth. It is not offered today, and nothing about it is a promise of revenue, outcomes, or adoption. When it stands, it will be stated here at the grade it has earned.

A standard implementation in every suite

0R10N15 is a standard implementation in every StegVerse suite. A suite is a determination about a system and what enables that system to provide reviewable evidence of its claims — and the protocol is stated identically in each suite, differing only where the suite states its instance. The suites are products of the same build this index describes; the protocol a suite carries is the protocol its crossings run under.
