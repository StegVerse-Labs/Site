1

Establish your node

Done by your device, here. Registering establishes your device's own StegOS node — its record lives in your browser, as on the StegOS Node page — and its reply equips you with the manifest generation and the submission instructions. It sends nothing to the seam; the seam's record is its ledger. You do this step once.

2

Build your manifest

The manifest says what you want done, and exactly how. It lists each file and its fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash). The automated builder can draft it from your stated purpose — you review and confirm it before anything is sent.

Download the submission kit — the file with the scaffold, the steps, and the rules.
The manifest, explained — the scaffold as a copyable card, what each field means, and worked examples.

3

Submit

Sending is two grants, both yours: deliver the manifest, and make your packet reachable at the locator it names. Your packet never travels and custody never moves — it is checked where it sits, against your manifest's fingerprints. There is no upload folder. The request itself is the work order, receipted from the moment it arrives. Three channels carry a manifest; use the one your surface already has:

1 · Ecosystem Chat. Paste your manifest into the chat — or paste the packet itself and answer "what do you want me to do with this data set?" Begin in Ecosystem Chat →

2 · Share from your storage. The seam’s inbox is a watched folder in a Google Drive owned by stegverse@gmail.com. Deposit your manifest in the inbox — the link is a standing approval to deposit (adding a file asks you to be signed in to any Google account; depositors can see one another’s manifests there). Or share the manifest file with stegverse@gmail.com from your own Drive. Keep your packet reachable at the locator your manifest names — if your files live on another cloud service, they can stay there; only the manifest travels. The deposit itself is the signal that starts the work. No email is needed or used by the process. Only manifests are worked — anything else deposited is held, unprocessed.

3 · Your node. If step 1 reads Device registered., place the manifest in your node's KV — it projects to the seam on its own.

Whichever channel you use, the seam receipts the same lineage: ingress, disposition, return.

Submissions are worked by the ecosystem’s lanes in their working sessions, in claim order. Expect a working-session turnaround, not an instant reply — your result waits for you in your storage when it lands.

4

Open your result

At submission you are answered with one link: your result file, named for the receipt that pins it. The file is not a document being edited — it is a projection. Open it, and it displays the replay of your run’s receipt segment, from the receipt pinned in its name to the current location in the process: the journey (every receipted step) and the governance (every disposition) exactly as they stand. Refresh it and the replay has moved as far as the run has.

The result is exactly where it is in the process. There is no finished document hiding behind the progress view — the replay is the result, at every point. Complete is just the final state transition in that replay, at the same link, under the same name. A person opens the file. A machine polls the link and reads its STATE and FINAL lines. Easy to follow, for anyone — or any machine.

Ask for the result anywhere. In your manifest, name a projection scope — other storage locations where this result may land. Anyone inside the scope can request it at their own location: the same bytes, byte-identical at every landing, each verifiable against the receipt. One run, seen by everyone you name — never the same run repeated for each reader.

Ask again, by receipt. Anyone holding a result’s receipt can submit a new manifest naming that receipt to request a replay — the run re-executed from its recorded inputs, its result declaring both receipts and whether the dispositions match — or a reconstruction: the run’s record reassembled from the ledger alone, every link verified.

The rules here

No credentials are held and no user data is collected — anything that asks you for either is not this seam. Custody of your files never moves. A file that arrives changed is a mismatch, not an arrival. An answer from this seam is a governed response with its receipt, a HOLD, or silence; anything else is not from here. The three buttons above act only on your own device — copying, downloading, and carrying you to Ecosystem Chat. This page has no forms and no trackers and collects nothing. Its scripts run on your device: the copy handler, and the Site's StegOS node stack behind the Register control — which keeps your device node's record in your browser, as that node's design intends. The seam's own record is never in your browser; it is the ledger at the seam.

What this page is. A picture of the record, not the record itself — the record is the ledger at the seam, and this page is generated from it (posture: MIRROR / RECEIPT_BACKED). If the two ever disagree, the disagreement is a finding: the page is corrected in a new generation, never quietly edited.
