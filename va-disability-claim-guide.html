The Guide and Claims Chat may explain procedures, identify evidence categories, organize user-confirmed facts, preserve uncertainty, and point to official sources. They do not adjudicate, provide medical opinions, guarantee ratings, act as an accredited representative, sign forms, or submit claims.

Phase 1 — Get access and preserve records

1Set up a secure VA.gov sign-in

Use Login.gov or ID.me. The guided workflow explains what each service is, what you need, and how to confirm the account works.

Open the guided account setup cards

2Download official records

Use the official VA medical-records page to obtain the complete available record, including the Blue Button download when applicable. Preserve an untouched original.

Open VA medical-record downloads

3Gather all claim-relevant evidence

Include supportive, unfavorable, conflicting, and incomplete evidence: service records, private and VA medical records, prior decisions, examinations, opinions, witness statements, employment records, symptom logs, and exposure records.

4Protect sensitive information

Records may contain Social Security numbers, addresses, diagnoses, financial information, and witness identities. Use trusted devices and networks, preserve originals, and disclose only what is necessary.

Phase 2 — Build the evidence map

  • Current diagnosis or persistent symptoms.
  • In-service event, injury, illness, exposure, or onset evidence.
  • Continuity and treatment history.
  • Nexus, secondary causation, or aggravation evidence from qualified sources.
  • Current severity and functional effects.
  • Lay and witness evidence.
  • Contrary, inconsistent, or unfavorable evidence.
  • Missing evidence and unresolved uncertainty.

Keep source facts, veteran statements, clinician conclusions, assistant inference, and uncertainty separate.

Phase 3 — Identify the claim path

  • Direct: current disability, in-service event or disease, and competent connection evidence.
  • Secondary: caused or aggravated by a service-connected disability or treatment.
  • Increase: an already service-connected condition has worsened.
  • Presumptive or exposure: current official eligibility requirements and qualifying service.
  • Aggravation: measurable worsening beyond the expected course.

Phase 4 — Prepare veteran-review materials

  • Evidence index for each claimed condition.
  • Dated timeline with source and page references.
  • Draft statements limited to confirmed facts.
  • Contradictions and unfavorable evidence shown rather than hidden.
  • Missing-evidence and next-action list.
  • Change log for drafted, revised, or flagged material.

Phase 5 — Filing gates

  1. Identity and account authority verified.
  2. Consent to process admitted records.
  3. Every material fact reviewed and confirmed.
  4. Every claimed condition selected by the veteran.
  5. Current forms, rules, and evidence requirements verified.
  6. Contradictions and unresolved items displayed.
  7. Exact package reviewed and authorized.
  8. Signature or valid delegation verified.
  9. Authorized VA or accredited-representative transport available.
  10. Submission confirmation and duplicate-prevention controls pass.

Until every applicable gate verifies, the system may prepare and review materials but must not submit the claim.
