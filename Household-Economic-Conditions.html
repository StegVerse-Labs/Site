Household Economic Conditions

A household-state view of the U.S. economy that separates gross earnings, usable resources, required costs, debt burden, necessary consumption, residual capacity, saving, borrowing, delinquency, and unmet need. Headline spending and aggregate growth are context—not substitutes for household welfare.

Breaks remain visible

Federal Reserve DSR
Current credit-bureau methodology is comparable from 2005 forward. The archived 1980–2024 prior-method series is a separate segment and must not be spliced into the current series.
New York Fed CCP
Main public household-debt continuity begins in 2003, with separately published 1999–2003 historical material. Student-loan data are reliable from 2003.
ACS
Standard 1-year comparisons begin in 2005 for this lane. The 2020 experimental 1-year release is noncomparable, and Census 2000 requires explicit table/universe/question review before historical comparison.
Interpretation boundary

What this page will not infer

  • Higher nominal spending does not prove increased physical consumption or need satisfaction.
  • Gross real weekly earnings do not establish net take-home resources or household usable capacity.
  • An aggregate debt-service ratio below a prior crisis peak does not establish low stress for indebted households or comparable debt composition.
  • GDP, productivity, or investment growth does not by itself establish household resilience or mobility.
